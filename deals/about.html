About

outdeals started as a simple idea: no ads, no affiliate cuts, no ranking algorithm. Brands and deal hunters outbid each other to rank #1 on a retailer's board — that's it.

Why a board per retailer

Shoppers arrive already knowing where they want to buy. Someone hunting an Amazon lightning deal is not in the market for a Walmart rollback, so a single blended list would have made every bid fight for the wrong attention. Four boards — Amazon, Target, Walmart and Altamuta — keep each bid competing only against deals a shopper is actually choosing between.

How the money works

A bid is a one-time payment for a position, not a subscription and not a cut of your sales. Raise your own listing and you pay only the difference between your old bid and your new one. Get outranked and your bid stays where it is until you decide to raise it — that is the whole business model, and there is nothing else behind it.

What this build is

This is a working demonstration clone, not a live marketplace. The leaderboard, the ranking rules, the raise-your-own-listing pricing and the activity feed all run for real — but entirely in your browser. Listings and bids are stored in localStorage, the starting board is seeded demo data, and the checkout step takes no card and moves no money. Anything you place is yours alone and vanishes when you clear site data.
